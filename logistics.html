Entry requirements

Visa Options

US citizens have two paths into China. Option A requires upfront paperwork but gives you a 10-year visa with no routing constraints. Option B is instant and free but requires careful planning around the onward-ticket rule.

Hard 10-day cap. No extensions.
Third-country onward ticket: For Option B, your exit flight must go to a country other than China and the US. A common solution: book an inexpensive one-way flight to Hong Kong, Japan, or Singapore as the exit leg, then a separate US return. This adds a small cost but keeps the trip visa-free.
All three families hold valid 10-year China visas from a previous trip — no visa action needed for anyone. Both options above are moot unless a visa expires before the trip.
Recommendation
If you're planning to return to China again in the next decade: get the L visa (Option A). The upfront effort is a one-time cost and you'll never think about this again. $140–252/person, 4 days processing, done.

If this is a one-time trip and you're comfortable booking a quick HKG or NRT hop as your exit leg, Option B works fine — just budget an extra day for the HK layover or treat it as a bonus stop.
