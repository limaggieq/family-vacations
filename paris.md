# Paris, France

## Pitch
The most visited city in the world — and it earns it. Art, food, and architecture at a density that makes every other city feel like it's still trying.

## Overview
Paris is the benchmark against which every other city gets measured. It has been the center of Western art, cuisine, fashion, and intellectual life for three centuries, and the infrastructure built around that centrality is extraordinary: the Louvre alone would take weeks to see fully, the restaurant scene has more Michelin stars than any other city, and the city's physical beauty — Haussmann's boulevards, the Seine, the arrondissement-by-arrondissement variety — is as consistent as advertised.

For a large family group with strollers and young kids, the key variable is neighborhood. Paris rewards careful base camp placement. The goal is to maximize stroller walking and minimize metro dependency. The right base makes a week feel effortless; the wrong one turns every outing into a logistics problem. St-Germain (6th) is the default — classic Parisian charm, walkable to the Left Bank, Luxembourg Gardens nearby. The 7th (Eiffel/Rue Cler area) is arguably better for stroller access: calmer streets, flat terrain, direct walk to Champ de Mars. Le Marais (3rd/4th) is flat and historic but can be noisy at night. The 1st (Louvre/Tuileries) is ultra-central but touristy. **Ranking for stroller-friendliness: 7th > St-Germain > Le Marais > 1st.**

## Culture
Parisian culture has a reputation for coldness that is partly earned and largely overstated. The French prioritize formality as a mark of respect — a "bonjour" before any request, a "merci" on exit — and visitors who observe these basics generally find the French helpful and warm. The city's actual culture is rich: café life is real, food is taken seriously at every level, and the French commitment to leisure — long lunches, Sunday closures — is a value worth experiencing.


## History
Paris has been continuously inhabited for over 2,000 years and has been central to European history for most of them. The French Revolution began here in 1789 and reshaped the political order of the Western world. The Impressionist movement emerged from Paris in the 1860s and changed how painting was understood. The city was occupied by Germany in 1940 and liberated in 1944. Notre-Dame, damaged by fire in 2019, reopened in late 2024 after restoration — seeing it again is one of the emotional experiences of the trip.

## Nature
Paris is a city of designed nature. The Luxembourg Gardens and the Tuileries are formal French gardens — geometric, manicured, and beautiful. The Jardin du Luxembourg has a beloved toy sailboat pond, a carousel, and a playground that has been the same experience for Parisian children for generations. The Seine river is the organizing spine of the city; the Promenade Les Berges de la Seine (river walk from Pont de l'Alma to Pont Royal) is one of the great urban walks anywhere.

Day trips reach genuine countryside: Versailles's formal gardens extend for kilometers behind the palace. Giverny — Claude Monet's home and the garden that inspired the Water Lilies — is 90 minutes by car through Normandy.

## Food
Paris is one of the three or four greatest food cities in the world. The croissant from a neighborhood boulangerie, eaten warm on the pavement, is a daily experience people come back specifically for. For the group: standout specific spots from the itinerary include Le Petit Cler (Rue Cler breakfast), La Citrouille (St-Germain bistro), Les Antiquaires (across from Musée d'Orsay), Bistrot Richelieu (Palais Royal), L'As du Fallafel (Marais, widely considered the best falafel in Paris), Creatures rooftop café at Galeries Lafayette. For a stay-in night: La Grande Épicerie de Paris at Le Bon Marché does cheese, charcuterie, ready-to-heat mains, and exceptional desserts.

## For Kids
- Jardin du Luxembourg — toy sailboats on the pond, carousel, and playground. The toy boat rental is a sensory memory of Paris that children carry for years.
- Eiffel Tower + Champ de Mars — the tower itself (pre-book summit tickets) and the park below, which has a playground and is perfect for a picnic.
- Palais Royal — the Colonnes de Buren (black-and-white striped columns in the courtyard) are an open sculpture playground. Kids immediately start jumping between them.
- Musée de l'Orangerie — Monet's Water Lilies in two immersive oval rooms. Bring coloring supplies for kids; the scale and the quiet are genuinely peaceful.
- Sainte-Chapelle — Gothic chapel with floor-to-ceiling stained glass on two levels. The upper chapel is one of the most beautiful interiors in France. Even young kids respond to the light.
- Bertillon ice cream (Île Saint-Louis) — the best ice cream in Paris, from a shop that has been operating since 1954. Queue is part of the experience.

## For the Family Group
- Both LAX and SEA have daily nonstops on Air France. Both crews land at CDG within ~90 minutes of each other — one of the cleanest arrival logistics on this list.
- Stroller-first base camp selection (St-Germain or 7th) eliminates metro dependency for most of the week. The Seine walk, Luxembourg Gardens, and Musée d'Orsay are all within stroller distance from either neighborhood.
- The itinerary is naturally structured by arrondissement — each day covers one neighborhood cluster, minimizing transit and maximizing walking. Kids do better with neighborhood days than landmark-hopping.
- Versailles and Giverny both work as full-day trips from St-Germain with minimal planning overhead.
- May is ideal: 66-69°F highs, sun sets at 9:30pm, crowds manageable, everything open.

## For Adults
- Musée d'Orsay — the Impressionist collection (Monet, Renoir, Van Gogh, Cézanne) in a converted railway station. Visit an hour before closing when crowds thin.
- Galeries Lafayette rooftop (Creatures café) — lunch with a panoramic Paris view. Unpretentious and excellent.
- Palais Garnier — the Paris opera house. Tour the interior even without attending a performance; the chandelier, the grand staircase, and the Marc Chagall-painted ceiling are extraordinary.
- Seine river cruise at night — Bateaux Mouches or a private boat. The illuminated bridges and monuments are Paris at its most cinematic.
- La Grande Épicerie de Paris — Paris's best food hall, at Le Bon Marché in St-Germain. For a stay-in dinner night: assemble a cheese and charcuterie board, a ready-to-heat main, salad, and dessert.

## Day Trips
- **Versailles** — 36 min by RER C from Saint-Michel Notre-Dame to Versailles Château Rive Gauche, then 15 min walk to the palace. Hall of Mirrors, formal gardens, golf cart or bike rental to cover the grounds. Book tickets well in advance. Full day.
- **Giverny** — 90 min by car or organized coach. Claude Monet's house and the water lily garden that inspired the paintings. Open April–November. Half day; can combine with a Normandy coast stop.

## Cost
**H — High.** Paris is among the most expensive cities on this list. Anchor hotel Citadines Saint-Germain-des-Prés (aparthotel format) is more family-friendly on price than traditional luxury hotels — rates run $400-650/night for a family unit with kitchen. Traditional luxury anchors (Le Bristol, Four Seasons George V) run $900-1,500+/night. Flights are competitive: Air France nonstops from both LAX and SEA run ~$800-1,100 roundtrip in peak season. Food ranges from cheap (boulangerie breakfast $5-10, falafel $12) to moderate (bistro dinner $45-70/person) to splurge. Museum entry $20-25/person; Versailles $25/person plus RER. Verify hotel rates at booking.

## Group Logistics
Citadines Saint-Germain-des-Prés (6th arrondissement) is the anchor — aparthotel format with full kitchens, multiple room configurations, and a location that puts Luxembourg Gardens, the Seine walk, and Musée d'Orsay all within stroller distance. Kitchen access matters for a large group: it enables easy breakfasts in, reduces the cost and logistical pressure of every meal being a restaurant event, and gives kids a place to decompress.

For 10+ people: book apartment-style units across 3-4 rooms. The Citadines format accommodates this well. Alternatively, a private apartment rental through Paris Attitude or Lodgis in St-Germain or the 7th gives the group a shared living space — better for a large family that wants to eat breakfast together.

Stroller note: St-Germain sidewalks can feel cramped on the busiest streets (Rue de Buci, Boulevard Saint-Germain). Side streets are generally fine. The 7th — if the group prefers — offers slightly more relaxed stroller access around Rue Cler and Champ de Mars.
